# UK ADHD research on emotional dysregulation, self-regulation and non-drug interventions, applied to "Apex"

Evidence-strength labels used: **Strong** (several meta-analyses of RCTs, including blinded outcomes), **Moderate** (meta-analyses with mostly unblinded or mixed-quality trials), **Weak** (small, uncontrolled or cross-sectional studies), **Theory** (a model or expert view, not an outcome trial).

Research note: PMC/Nature full texts were blocked during this session (captcha or 403 errors), so some figures come from abstracts and search snippets rather than full papers. Where that matters, it is flagged.

## 1. Emotional dysregulation in childhood ADHD: how it is described, how common it is, what helps

### Takeaway
Emotional dysregulation (ED) is common in ADHD but not universal. It affects roughly a quarter to a half of children with ADHD, predicts worse outcomes, and is increasingly treated as a core or associated feature rather than a side issue. Among non-drug options, the best-supported help for children is relationship-based (parent training, social skills). Child-only emotion skills training has a thinner evidence base. Apex's focus on ED is well justified. Its claim to *teach* regulation should be framed modestly.

### Cited Findings
- Shaw, Stringaris, Nigg & Leibenluft (2014, *Am J Psychiatry*; Stringaris then at King's College London) estimate ED in **about 25–45% of children** and 30–70% of adults with ADHD. Clinic studies of young people give 24–50%. Overlap is "far from complete": many children with ADHD do not have impairing ED **(Moderate, review)**. — [Shaw et al. 2014, Am J Psychiatry](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2013.13070966); [figure on prevalence](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Prevalence-Estimates-of-Emotion-Dysregulation-in-Children-and-Adults-With-ADHD-a_tbl2_259988529)
- ED "represents a major source of impairment and presages a poor clinical outcome". In one longitudinal study of 79 children with ADHD, those with ED had more comorbidity, more social impairment and more persistent ADHD four years later. — [Shaw et al. 2014 (Focus reprint)](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.focus.140102)
- In adult ADHD, UK work (Hirsch, Chavanon, Riechmann & Asherson's group) argues ED is a *primary* symptom of ADHD, not just a result of comorbidity **(Moderate, adult data)**. — [ScienceDirect: "Emotional dysregulation is a primary symptom in adult ADHD"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165032717323662); [systematic review: ED as a core symptom of adult ADHD](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9821724/)
- An ACAMH practitioner blog (Prof Aja Murray, Edinburgh, & Amanda Russell, 13 Sept 2026) gives evidence-informed tips for adolescents with ADHD: acceptance, cognitive reappraisal, managing rumination with "if…then" plans, **grounding (5-4-3-2-1 senses)**, "riding the wave" (DBT/mindfulness urge tolerance) and sleep. It cites Sobanski et al. 2010, Graziano & Garcia 2016 (meta-analysis linking ADHD to ED), Fisher et al. 2022 (sleep and emotion regulation) and Materna 2019 / Thorell 2020 (reappraisal). It does **not** cover co-regulation or breathing as separate strategies **(expert guidance)**. — [ACAMH blog](https://www.acamh.org/blog/supporting-emotion-regulation-in-adolescents-with-adhd-adhd-symptoms/)
- Meta-analysis (Guo, Assumpcao & Hu, *J Atten Disord* 2021/22, not UK authors): for **children**, therapies aimed at the child's relationships worked on emotional symptoms after treatment. These were parent training (for ED and depression) and social skills training (for ED). "Efficacy… varies substantially across children and adults" **(Moderate)**. — [Guo et al., J Atten Disord](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/10870547211001953)
- A 2026 JCPP paper (Bohner et al.) looked at emotional problems and ED as predictors and moderators in an RCT of methylphenidate versus neurofeedback. This shows ED is now a live outcome and moderator in trials (abstract only seen). — [Bohner et al. 2026, JCPP](https://acamh.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jcpp.70205)

### Inferences
- Apex's "rage tells you something matters" framing fits acceptance-based approaches (ACT/DBT "allowing emotions without self-judgement") that UK practitioner guidance recommends.
- Apex's "One Thing" tool is a shortened version of the 5-4-3-2-1 grounding technique in ACAMH guidance. That gives it practitioner face validity. Its *efficacy evidence* in children with ADHD specifically is missing (see section 4).
- Because only some children with ADHD have marked ED, experts might ask whether the six instincts fit this child's actual profile. The parent's personalisation (the child's own warning signs) partly addresses this.

### Gaps
- I could not open the full texts of the named UK researchers' work on childhood ED (for example Sayal, Ford, Hill, Michelini). Michelini's EEG/ERP work on emotional lability and Hill's work on irritability were not retrieved.
- I found no UK RCT of a child-delivered digital emotion-regulation game for ADHD.

## 2. Sonuga-Barke's dual-pathway / delay-aversion model: implications for "Wait for the Signal" and delayed rewards

### Takeaway
The model holds that many children with ADHD find waiting itself *aversive*: delay creates frustration and arousal, and the child acts to escape it. Long, fixed waits with delayed rewards are therefore exactly the conditions that trigger ADHD behaviour. The model also suggests a remedy: build delay tolerance gradually (shaping, fading, graded exposure) and reduce unnecessary delay. Apex's 13–21-second waits and late-confirmed rewards should therefore be graded and short at first, and the waiting should be filled with something to do **(Theory with lab support)**.

### Cited Findings
- Dual-pathway model: ADHD comes from two largely independent deficits. One is executive/inhibitory dysfunction (dorsal fronto-striatal circuits). The other is **delay aversion**, a motivational style of escaping or avoiding delay (reward circuits, steep discounting of delayed outcomes, "active distaste for waiting"). — [Sonuga-Barke, Psychological heterogeneity in AD/HD: a dual pathway model](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11496846_Psychological_heterogeneity_in_ADHD_-_A_dual_pathway_model_of_behaviour_and_cognition); [ECNU summary](https://jps.ecnu.edu.cn/EN/Y2012/V35/I4/1018)
- "The imposition of fixed delay creates frustration and emotional arousal and leads to attempts to modify the experience of waiting." ADHD behaviours are context-dependent and get stronger as delay increases. — [Delay Aversion and ADHD (core.ac.uk)](https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/12827912.pdf)
- Implications drawn by the delay-aversion literature: "modify current settings to limit unnecessary delay where possible" and "increase delay tolerance… perhaps through **shaping and fading procedures** or… desensitization through **gradual exposure**." — [Delay Aversion and ADHD](https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/12827912.pdf)
- Preschool children with ADHD showed both inhibitory deficits and more delay aversion than controls. This supports the motivational/cognitive split. — [Sonuga-Barke et al., PubMed 15303301](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15303301/)
- Sonuga-Barke, Bitsakou & Thorell (2010) expanded the model to a *triple* pathway, adding timing deficits that separate from inhibition and delay. — [Beyond the dual pathway model](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/43247877_Beyond_the_Dual_Pathway_Model_Evidence_for_the_Dissociation_of_Timing_Inhibitory_and_Delay-Related_Impairments_in_Attention-DeficitHyperactivity_Disorder)
- Delay aversion in ADHD involves **amygdala** and prefrontal hyper-activation during cued delays (Van Dessel et al. 2018, JCPP, Sonuga-Barke group). That makes waiting an *emotional* event, not just a cognitive one. — [Van Dessel et al. 2018](https://acamh.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jcpp.12868)
- Delay aversion is "neuro-biologically rooted" but "exacerbated by early negative social interactions during waiting-related encounters". A 2025 longitudinal JCPP study looked at maternal influences on its preschool origins. — [Are ADHD trajectories shaped by the social environment? (JCPP 2025, PubMed 39710599)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39710599/)

### Inferences
- **Wait for the Signal** fits the model well *if* waits start short and lengthen gradually (shaping/fading), and if failures are not punished. Apex's "consequence, never game over" helps. Fixed 13–21-second waits from the start may be long for a delay-averse 9-year-old. An expert might suggest starting at 3–5 seconds and adapting to the child's performance.
- Filling the wait (the rising-urge waves, the breathing) matches the model's point that children try to "modify the experience of waiting". Giving them a sanctioned activity during the wait is consistent with this.
- **Delayed rewards** (missions confirmed later, trophies) cut against delay aversion. Experts would likely recommend immediate, frequent feedback (instant visual or sound reward at the moment of success), with any later reward added on top.
- Because negative social experiences around waiting may worsen delay aversion, the parent's tone during waits ("do it with them") matters. That supports the co-regulation design.

### Gaps
- I found no RCT that specifically trains delay tolerance with a game in school-age children with ADHD. Graded-delay ideas remain theoretical or lab-based.

## 3. EAGG meta-analyses: cognitive training, neurofeedback, behavioural interventions, mindfulness; blinded vs unblinded outcomes; transfer

### Takeaway
The European ADHD Guidelines Group consistently finds the same pattern. Non-drug interventions look effective when rated by people who know the child received them (usually parents), but symptom effects shrink or vanish with **probably blinded** raters. Behavioural parent training is the exception for *non-symptom* outcomes: it has blinded evidence for better parenting and fewer conduct problems. Training gains (for example working memory) generally **do not transfer** to everyday ADHD symptoms. Parent-reported improvement after playing Apex therefore cannot be read as proof that it works **(Strong)**.

### Cited Findings
- **Cognitive training** (Cortese, Ferrin, Brandeis, Buitelaar, Daley… Sonuga-Barke; *JAACAP* 2015): with probably blinded outcomes there was **no effect on total ADHD symptoms** (SMD 0.12, 95% CI −0.01 to 0.25) and none on hyperactivity/impulsivity (SMD 0.12). Training improved working memory performance but had "limited effects on ADHD symptoms". Effects were sizeable with unblinded raters and "drastically reduced" with active controls or blinded raters. — [KCL Pure record](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/cognitive-training-for-attention-deficithyperactivity-disorder-me/); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/272819688_Cognitive_Training_for_Attention-DeficitHyperactivity_Disorder_Meta-Analysis_of_Clinical_and_Neuropsychological_Outcomes_From_Randomized_Controlled_Trials)
- **Computerised cognitive training** update (Westwood, Parlatini, Rubia, Cortese, Sonuga-Barke; *Molecular Psychiatry* 2023): a meta-analysis restricted to RCTs with *blinded and objective* outcomes. Its title and design confirm the EAGG focus on blinding. I could not open the full text to check effect sizes. My recollection, *to verify*, is that it found working-memory gains and small or no effects on blinded ADHD symptoms. — [Nature/Mol Psychiatry](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-023-02000-7); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10208955/)
- **Neurofeedback** (Cortese et al., EAGG, *JAACAP* 2016): "Evidence from well-controlled trials with probably blinded outcomes currently fails to support neurofeedback as an effective treatment for ADHD." — [PubMed 27238063](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27238063/?dopt=Abstract); [National Elf Service commentary](https://www.nationalelfservice.net/mental-health/adhd/neurofeedback-for-adhd-in-children/)
- **Behavioural interventions** (Daley, van der Oord, Ferrin, Danckaerts, Doepfner, Cortese, Sonuga-Barke; *JAACAP* 2014): there was no blinded evidence of fewer ADHD symptoms, **but there was blinded evidence of better parenting and fewer child conduct problems**. There were also possible gains in parenting self-concept, but not in parental mental wellbeing. — [PubMed 25062591](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25062591/)
- A 2025 JCPP Editorial Perspective, "The challenge of evaluating ADHD parenting interventions – towards a hybrid approach", continues the blinding debate. Parenting interventions are hard to blind, and the outcomes parents value may need parent reports. — [PubMed 41123338](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41123338/)
- **Mindfulness**: recent meta-analyses (mostly not EAGG) report moderate symptom improvements in children. Reviewers caution that "methodological quality of the studies reviewed is low", and that family-based mindfulness ranked highest in one network meta-analysis **(Weak–Moderate)**. — [Bayesian MA of MBIs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13013061/); [MBI systematic review/MA](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9690476/); [Network MA (Cureus 2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12947048/)
- NICE (NG87) evidence reviews on non-drug ADHD treatment are the UK guideline basis. — [NICE evidence review, NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK578098/)

### Inferences
- Apex is closest to "cognitive/skills training delivered via a game". Mechanically it resembles the in-game skill practice that EAGG finds does not transfer to blinded everyday symptoms. Experts would likely question **transfer**: does practising Long Exhale in the game get used in a real meltdown at school? Apex's design choice that the tools are "performed for real" (actual breathing, actual covering of eyes) and practised with a parent is the strongest defence. The parent-coaching side is also where the evidence is best.
- Any evaluation by the parent is unblinded. A UK expert would ask for an objective or third-party marker (for example a teacher who does not know about the game, or a count of incidents) before claiming benefit.
- Apex is better framed as a *vehicle for parent–child practice and shared language* (an area with blinded support for parenting and conduct outcomes) than as a standalone treatment.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the exact effect sizes from Westwood et al. 2023.
- I found no EAGG meta-analysis dedicated to mindfulness in children with ADHD.

## 4. Breathing / slow exhale, mindfulness, interoception and grounding in children with ADHD (including Rubia's work)

### Takeaway
There is plausible physiology and practitioner support for slow breathing, grounding and interoception, but **direct trial evidence in children with ADHD is weak**. Katya Rubia's meditation work (Sahaja Yoga, a family programme) showed promising parent-rated gains, but in an uncontrolled, unblinded study. Interoception research suggests that people with ADHD are *less* accurate at reading body signals. That supports teaching warning signs, but also means children may struggle to notice them **(Weak)**.

### Cited Findings
- Harrison, Manocha & Rubia (Sahaja Yoga Meditation as a family treatment programme for children with ADHD, *Clin Child Psychol Psychiatry*, 2004; KCL). Parents and children meditated together twice weekly. Parent-rated ADHD symptoms improved by about 35%, along with self-esteem and parent–child relationship quality. — [KCL Pure](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/sahaja-yoga-meditation-as-a-family-treatment-programme-for-children-with-attention-deficithyperactivity-disorder(b4f76c45-125c-49bf-8a60-635295b43a0c).html); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Sahaja-Yoga-Meditation-as-a-Family-Treatment-for-Harrison-Manocha/584173bf5efb0045e330f54c7c0348377873a9d2)
- Limitation: that study "relies on unblinded ratings of parents and lacked a control group" **(Weak)**. Note it predates the 2015–2026 window. — [Systematic review of yoga and meditation for ADHD in children (PMC10101238)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10101238/)
- Reviews of yoga, mindfulness and meditation in young people with ADHD: "preliminary findings suggest… may be beneficial… extensive research is required." — [Springer J Child Fam Stud 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10826-018-1148-7); [Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry 2018 systematic review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00787-017-1008-9)
- Interoception: a systematic review (2025, PMC11842156) reports **diminished interoceptive awareness in ADHD**. People with ADHD were less accurate in heartbeat detection and tended to underestimate heart rate (snippet only; full text blocked). — [Systematic review, PMC11842156](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11842156/); [CHADD summary](https://chadd.org/attention-article/physiological-and-emotional-awareness-in-individuals-with-adhd/)
- In typically developing young people, better cardiac interoceptive accuracy is linked to better emotion regulation and less use of maladaptive strategies (ages 9–16). Interoception-based programmes improved emotion regulation in autistic children, and authors suggest they may transfer to ADHD **(Weak, extrapolated)**. — [Getting to the heart of emotion regulation in youth](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5065133/); [Interoception-based programme in autistic children](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9045986/)
- Grounding (5-4-3-2-1) appears in UK practitioner guidance for adolescents with ADHD. — [ACAMH blog 2026](https://www.acamh.org/blog/supporting-emotion-regulation-in-adolescents-with-adhd-adhd-symptoms/)

### Inferences
- Apex's design of the child choosing 3 personal warning signs, plus "prompt earlier" after a takeover, is a sensible scaffold for weak interoception: external prompts stand in for internal signals. Experts might suggest pairing the child's self-report with a parent-observed cue, because children with ADHD may under-notice bodily arousal.
- A meter that falls only on the out-breath (3 in, 5 out) is a reasonable child-friendly biofeedback-like mechanic. Longer exhales are a widely used calming technique. I found **no ADHD-specific RCT of slow exhale breathing**, so it is best described as low-risk and plausible, not evidence-based for ADHD.
- Rubia's work being *family-based* (parent and child together) matches Apex's "do it with them" approach.

### Gaps
- I found no UK trial of paced breathing or HRV-biofeedback in children with ADHD.
- I could not access Rubia's newer mindfulness/meditation neuroimaging work.

## 5. Behavioural parent training in the UK (NFPP, Incredible Years): what it teaches and alignment with shared phrases / "do it with them"

### Takeaway
Behavioural parent training (BPT) is the UK's first-line non-drug approach for younger children (NICE NG87). It has blinded evidence for better parenting and fewer conduct problems, and evidence that it helps children's emotional symptoms. The New Forest Parenting Programme (Sonuga-Barke, Thompson, Daley) specifically targets ADHD self-regulation through parent-led games, scaffolding and consistent cues. Apex's shared phrases and joint practice fit this model well **(Moderate–Strong for parenting and conduct outcomes)**.

### Cited Findings
- NFPP is a home-delivered, ADHD-specific programme for preschoolers. Sonuga-Barke et al. 2001 (58 families) reported less hyperactivity and fewer conduct problems, and more parenting satisfaction, at 23-week follow-up. — [EIF Guidebook entry (July 2024)](https://guidebook.eif.org.uk/files/pdfs/programmes-the-new-forest-parenting-programme.pdf); [Thompson et al. 2009 small RCT (NTU)](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/46633/)
- In an evaluation of NFPP, parent-rated ADHD symptoms fell, and behaviour in a play observation task improved as rated by **blinded observers**. — [EIF Guidebook](https://guidebook.eif.org.uk/files/pdfs/programmes-the-new-forest-parenting-programme.pdf)
- A UK multicentre RCT (Sonuga-Barke et al. 2018) compared NFPP with Incredible Years for preschool ADHD. NFPP cost less per family (£1591 vs £2103). Neither was clearly superior (from the publication record; full text not seen). — [Bangor University record](https://research.bangor.ac.uk/en/publications/a-comparison-of-the-clinical-effectiveness-and-cost-of-specialize/); [Trial protocol, Trials 2014](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1745-6215-15-142)
- BPT gives blinded improvements in parenting and conduct problems (EAGG). — [Daley et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25062591/)
- Long-term meta-analysis: BPT gains are sustained for ADHD symptoms, behaviour problems, positive parenting, parenting competence and parent–child relationship quality. — [Sustained improvements by BPT, PMC10501699](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10501699/)
- Parent training improves children's ED and emotional symptoms (meta-analysis). — [Guo et al.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/10870547211001953)

### Inferences
- From my background knowledge of NFPP (not verified in this session): NFPP teaches parents to use eye contact, short and consistent cue phrases, praise, scaffolding and attention/waiting *games* to build self-control. Apex's "shared phrases" (for example "later", "out 5") and parent-joined practice match this. The game could be presented as a *tool inside* parent-led co-regulation, not a replacement for it.
- Repair scenes after takeovers match parent-training emphasis on keeping a warm relationship after conflict.
- Experts would note that BPT evidence is strongest for preschoolers. At age 9, school-age BPT and CBT-informed approaches are also relevant.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve NFPP manual content directly, so the specific techniques listed are from background knowledge.
- I found no UK study of a parent-plus-game hybrid.

## 6. Shame, self-esteem, and deficit vs strengths ("superpower") framing

### Takeaway
UK leaders (Sonuga-Barke, Thapar) take the neurodiversity concept seriously. They see impairment as partly context-dependent and favour environmental accommodation, but they also caution against dropping the clinical view of real impairment. Framing each instinct as having an upside fits the "neurodivergence" view. A UK expert would likely warn against implying the difficulties are *only* strengths, or that ADHD is a "superpower" **(Theory / expert opinion)**.

### Cited Findings
- Sonuga-Barke & Thapar (2021), "The neurodiversity concept: is it helpful for clinicians and scientists?", *Lancet Psychiatry* 8:559–561. The title itself is a question. They engage critically rather than simply endorsing the concept. — [ORCA Cardiff](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/143266/); [KCL Pure](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/the-neurodiversity-concept-is-it-helpful-for-clinicians-and-scien/)
- Sonuga-Barke (2023, JCPP Editorial Perspective) frames ADHD as neuro-divergence in the digital world. It replaces the "dysfunction within the individual" assumption with neurodivergence, and stresses that impairment is context-dependent and can be eased through "reasonable accommodation". — [JCPP 2023](https://acamh.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jcpp.13710); [Frontiers discussion](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnbeh.2022.1041865/full)
- Participatory, neurodivergence-informed research models for ADHD and autism have been proposed in the *British Journal of Psychiatry*. — [BJPsych](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-british-journal-of-psychiatry/article/participatory-translational-science-of-neurodivergence-model-for-attentiondeficithyperactivity-disorder-and-autism-research/9ADA154B09817F10BB55A9115B391F1D)
- Rubia's family meditation study reported self-esteem improvements (unblinded). — [KCL Pure](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/sahaja-yoga-meditation-as-a-family-treatment-programme-for-children-with-attention-deficithyperactivity-disorder(b4f76c45-125c-49bf-8a60-635295b43a0c).html)

### Inferences
- "Rage is not bad, it tells you something matters" and "strengths, not deficits" sit well with acceptance-based regulation and with the neurodivergence framing. "Consequence, never game over" plus repair also lowers shame.
- A balanced line that matches UK expert views would be: "these instincts have upsides *and* can cause real problems; tools help you choose". Avoid "superpower". The game's design of instincts that "take over" already acknowledges real impairment, which is a good balance.

### Gaps
- I did not find a specific UK empirical study of how "superpower" messaging affects children's self-esteem. Cautions here are expert opinion, not trial data.
- Shame and self-esteem in ADHD (for example research on rejection sensitivity) were not retrieved.

## 7. Exercise and movement breaks

### Takeaway
Meta-analyses (mostly non-UK, many from China) report moderate-to-large benefits of structured exercise on executive function and some ADHD symptoms in children. Trial quality varies and effect sizes are probably inflated. Acute single bouts also show short-term benefits. "Burst & Rest" with movement breaks is consistent with this evidence and is low-risk **(Moderate, with quality caveats)**.

### Cited Findings
- Sun et al. 2024 (*Child: Care, Health and Development*), n=627: structured physical exercise had a large effect on executive function (SMD 2.19), response inhibition (1.99) and selective attention (1.70), and also reduced ADHD symptoms. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/cch.13150)
- PLOS ONE 2023 meta-analysis: physical activity improved inhibitory control (SMD −0.50), working memory (−0.50) and cognitive flexibility. Intensity and volume moderated the effects. — [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0289732)
- A single bout of exercise improved core symptoms and executive functions (Chen & Zhu 2024, *J Atten Disord*). — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/10870547231217321)
- Network meta-analysis: ball-based aerobic exercise ranked best for inhibitory control. — [PMC10080114](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10080114/)

### Inferences
- An SMD of 2.19 is implausibly large. It suggests small, heterogeneous, unblinded trials, which is the same blinding concern EAGG raises. Experts would accept movement breaks as sensible and low-risk, but not as a proven treatment.
- Short in-game movement breaks are "single bouts", the format with supportive acute evidence.

### Gaps
- I did not identify a UK-led exercise trial for childhood ADHD, or an EAGG meta-analysis on exercise.
